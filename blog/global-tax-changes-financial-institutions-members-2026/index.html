A Global Financial System in Transition

The way money moves is changing. So is the way governments regulate, tax, and monitor financial activity.

Digital commerce crosses borders at unprecedented speed. Businesses offer services in markets where they have no physical presence. Governments are expanding tax obligations, electronic reporting, and transaction transparency.

Stripe's 2026 Tax Trends and Changes report captures the breadth of that transition.

Stripe reports that the first-half 2025 U.S. rate-change count was nearly 25% higher than the same period of 2024. Across Europe, Poland, Belgium and France advanced national e-invoicing mandates; Asia-Pacific markets have been developing varied reporting models; and Brazil is restructuring its consumption tax regime. These changes do not impose identical obligations on every institution, but they point toward a more information-intensive financial system.

How can institutions modernize for growing complexity while creating greater financial opportunities for the people they serve?

The Institutional Challenge: Complexity Without Losing Control

Financial institutions are evaluating digital assets, tokenized instruments, programmable transactions and alternative settlement networks. Each capability creates operational considerations beyond payment speed: who may authorize a transaction, what controls apply, which risks must be evaluated, and what evidence will remain afterward?

For a credit union offering digital-asset-backed credit, market value alone does not necessarily tell the full story. Positions may carry unrealized gains, losses and other tax-relevant characteristics. Institutions need a way to consider appropriate information without surrendering their governance responsibilities.

Why the Member Matters Just as Much as the Institution

Credit unions differentiate themselves through member relationships and financial well-being. Technology that strengthens institutional oversight should also create the opportunity for better member decisions.

Imagine a member with both Bitcoin and Ethereum holdings: one position with substantial unrealized gains, another showing a loss. If the institution chooses to offer appropriate member-facing insights, a member could potentially understand tax-relevant position characteristics and explore collateral options rather than relying on market balances alone.

The Member Wins

Greater visibility into asset positions, more informed borrowing decisions and the possibility of accessing liquidity without immediately selling long-term holdings. Actual access and benefits depend on the institution's offering and individual circumstances.

The Institution Wins

More informed collateral evaluation, structured risk controls, auditable decisions and the opportunity to extend member relationships into responsibly governed digital-asset services.

From Global Tax Complexity to More Intelligent Infrastructure

The matters covered in Stripe's report and the function of Aetherum's TPS are different. Stripe addresses indirect taxes—sales tax, VAT, GST—and reporting requirements such as electronic invoicing. TPS addresses tax-relevant characteristics of digital-asset collateral positions; it is not a VAT, sales-tax or invoicing compliance engine.

They nevertheless illustrate a shared direction: financial infrastructure increasingly needs better information, clear accountability and records that explain not only what moved, but how decisions were made.

The Next Opportunity for Credit Unions and Community Banks

No institution needs to offer every digital-asset product immediately. The strategic question is whether it can evaluate emerging services responsibly, preserve institutional control and deliver meaningful member value when opportunities arise.

Aetherum's control-plane architecture and DACS technology reflect a long-term commitment to that model: more informed institutions, better-informed members and responsible pathways into digital finance.

The next generation of financial infrastructure should do more than accommodate regulatory change. It should help financial institutions serve their members better.
