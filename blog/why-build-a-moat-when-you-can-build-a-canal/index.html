The Architecture

Aetherum is an audit-native lending stack for NCUA-regulated credit unions. Every feature is designed around a single question: Can I explain this to an examiner? This shapes everything.

Risk Assessment Model

At the center of Aetherum is our risk assessment model (DACS) — an AI-driven underwriting engine fine-tuned on 963 NCUA underwriting examples. DACS doesn't score credit; it produces audit-ready compliance evidence. Every risk signal is mapped to NCUA guidance, every factor is traceable, and every decision is timestamped and locked into the audit trail.

The DACS score feeds into a member's qualified loan amount — the maximum they can borrow against their crypto holdings. But here's the key: DACS produces not just a number, but a narrative. An NCUA examiner can read the exact factors that moved the dial: collateral volatility, member tenure, loan-to-value ratio, market conditions. This is not credit scoring dressed up as compliance. This is compliance-first underwriting from the architecture up.

Pre-Signing Transaction Verification (AetherumTxGuard)

No blockchain transaction leaves Aetherum without inspection. AetherumTxGuard sits between every loan repayment, collateral movement, and liquidation decision and the signing key. Before any transaction reaches the custody layer, TxGuard decodes the raw EVM calldata, evaluates it against the credit union's own configurable policy rules, and fires an immutable log to the on-chain audit trail.

Every CU sets their own transaction policy: which protocols they trust, which functions they'll allow, what value ceiling they accept, whether they require dual approval for large moves. That policy is evaluated every single time — before the signature key ever sees the request. If the transaction violates policy, it's blocked and logged. If it passes, it's approved and logged. An NCUA examiner can pull the entire TxGuard report and see: this transaction passed our rules, this one was blocked and here's why, this one required manual approval.

This is not just a risk control. It's compliance infrastructure.

Multi-Wallet Identity Binding

Credit union members hold crypto across multiple custodians — MetaMask for their personal holdings, Coinbase for another slice, and maybe they're rolling off an older wallet they lost the seed phrase for. Today, if they want to borrow against any of it, they have to move everything to one place. Or they do multiple loans against the same credit union. Both paths create operational friction and compliance complexity.

One Member, Many Wallets changes this. A credit union member has a persistent identity in the Aetherum system, and that identity can bind to multiple approved wallets without re-KYC'ing each time. Rotate a key? Your identity stays. Recover a lost wallet? Link the new one. Switch custodians? Add the new custodian's wallet to your identity without restarting the underwriting process.

The binding is on-chain (ERC-3643 compliant) and encrypted. Only the wallet addresses and their compliance status are visible on-chain; the member's identity and funds are not. An NCUA examiner can see the full identity→wallet graph and verify that all wallets in a member's portfolio are approved and that every loan is against the same identity.

CU Debt Instruments

Credit unions are asset-liability managers. The loan products they originate should feed into secondary market instruments that CUs can issue, hold, and sell. CU Debt Instruments let a credit union issue a bond-like instrument backed by a pool of Aetherum loans, with an escrow-funded coupon cycle and a triggerable liquidation gate.

But the liquidation gate is not in the CU's hands alone. It's gated by TxGuard. If the collateral LTV breaches 90%, or if a coupon payment is missed, Aetherum evaluates the liquidation decision through the CU's own transaction policy before anything moves. This ensures that even in a crisis, the transaction that triggers the sell is logged, authorized, and explainable.
